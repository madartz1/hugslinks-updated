Apartment Search

Searching for housing with assistance

Finding a listing is only part of the process. Before paying money or submitting sensitive information, verify the apartment, landlord or broker and understand any subsidy approval or inspection requirements.

1. Know your housing budget Understand the rent level your household or subsidy may support.
2. Verify the listing Confirm the apartment and person offering it are legitimate.
3. Prepare documents Keep digital and paper copies of important paperwork.
4. Confirm approval requirements Some assisted apartments require program review or inspection before move-in.

HUGS Housing Explainer

What does this mean for me?

Government housing language can be difficult to understand. HUGSLinks organizes common situations into plain-language explanations and practical next steps.

CityFHEPS

Rental assistance may expand your apartment options.

What matters

CityFHEPS rules, rent levels, eligibility and housing-search procedures can change.

What this may mean for you

If you already have CityFHEPS or are being evaluated for it, keep your household and program documents current before apartment hunting.

Next step

Confirm your current household size, program status and approved rental information before committing to a unit.

Housing Connect

One rejection does not mean every development is out of reach.

What matters

Each affordable development can have different income and household rules.

What this may mean for you

You may qualify for one development even if another does not fit your income or household size.

Next step

Keep your Housing Connect profile current and continue applying to opportunities that fit your household.

Section 8

A closed waiting list does not always mean an existing application disappeared.

What matters

Waiting lists, existing applications and special admissions can operate differently.

What this may mean for you

If you already applied, continue checking your official application status rather than assuming your case ended.

Next step

Keep your address, phone number, email and household information current.

Housing Stability

You may be able to seek help before entering shelter.

What matters

Prevention programs exist for some households experiencing housing instability.

What this may mean for you

If you are behind on rent, received eviction papers or lost income, you may have options before homelessness occurs.

Next step

Gather your lease, rent records, court papers, income information and benefits documents before seeking help.

Supportive Housing

Some households may qualify for housing with support services.

What matters

Supportive housing generally requires eligibility assessment and provider involvement.

What this may mean for you

Certain health, disability, homelessness or support needs may make supportive housing worth exploring.

Next step

Ask an appropriate case manager, health provider or social-service provider whether an assessment may be appropriate.

Housing Rights

A voucher should not automatically end your apartment search.

What matters

Housing protections can apply to lawful sources of income, including certain rental subsidies.

What this may mean for you

If a landlord or broker refuses to consider you because of a lawful subsidy, there may be housing-rights resources available.

Next step

Save advertisements, screenshots, messages, broker information and other records if you believe discrimination occurred.
