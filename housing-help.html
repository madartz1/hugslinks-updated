Apartment Search

Searching for housing with assistance

Finding a listing is only part of the process. Before paying money or submitting sensitive information, verify the listing, landlord or broker and understand any subsidy approval or inspection requirements.

1. Know your housing budget Understand the rent level your household or subsidy may support.
2. Verify the listing Confirm the apartment and the person offering it are legitimate.
3. Prepare documents Keep digital and paper copies of important paperwork.
4. Confirm approval Assisted apartments may require program review or inspection.

HUGS Housing Explainer

What does this mean for me?

Government housing information can be difficult to understand. Use these plain-language starting points to identify what may be relevant to your situation and what to verify next.

CityFHEPS

Rental assistance may expand your housing options.

What matters

CityFHEPS rules, rent levels, eligibility and housing-search procedures can change.

What this may mean for you

If you have CityFHEPS or are being evaluated for it, keep your household and program information current.

Next step

Confirm your current program status, household size and approved rental information before committing to a unit.

Housing Connect

One development may not determine every opportunity.

What matters

Affordable developments can have different household and income requirements.

What this may mean for you

You may qualify for one development even if another does not match your household.

Next step

Keep your Housing Connect profile updated and continue reviewing matching opportunities.

Section 8

A waitlist change may affect different applicants differently.

What matters

Waiting lists, existing applications and voucher issuance can have different rules.

What this may mean for you

If you already applied, continue checking official application information rather than assuming your case ended.

Next step

Keep your address, phone number, email and household information current.

Housing Stability

Help may be available before homelessness occurs.

What matters

Housing-prevention programs exist for some households facing instability.

What this may mean for you

Falling behind on rent or receiving eviction papers may be a reason to seek prevention assistance early.

Next step

Gather your lease, rent records, court papers, income information and benefits documentation.

Supportive Housing

Some households may qualify for housing with support services.

What matters

Supportive housing generally involves eligibility assessment and provider participation.

What this may mean for you

Certain disability, health, homelessness or support circumstances may make this pathway worth exploring.

Next step

Ask an appropriate provider or case manager whether supportive housing assessment may be appropriate.

Housing Rights

Rental assistance should not automatically end your apartment search.

What matters

Housing protections may apply to lawful sources of income, including qualifying subsidies.

What this may mean for you

A refusal based specifically on lawful rental assistance may raise a housing-rights issue.

Next step

Save advertisements, messages, broker information and other records if you believe discrimination occurred.
